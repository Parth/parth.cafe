At the current moment, somehow AI has managed to be something that is both the subject of outrage, and immense excitement.

This is going to be an attempt to index the various perspectives I've heard and share my perspective. 

# Outrage

## Energy Usage
AI energy usage. This reminds me of the stuff that was going on with Proof of Work. The people consuming the energy paid for that energy. I think it would be a more sound argument for the outrage to be directed towards how that energy is produced or towards any cronyism taking place.

## Circular Finance
The financial bubble. To me this seems likely, but also not a moral issue. We have the freedom to short these companies. I too would be outraged if the government bailed out AI companies, but my outrage would be mostly directed to the government. If you're upset about large institutions exposing themselves to AI, you can just move your money elsewhere.

## IP
Intellectual Property. Now things are getting more compelling for me. It seems a shame that much of the training data was produced by people that won't be compensated for their efforts. [StackOverflow](https://meta.stackoverflow.com/questions/437921/how-does-the-continued-decline-in-posts-since-may-25-influence-our-interpretati) is the most clear example of this. SO was a huge contributor to training data for these LLMs, and LLMs will be a huge contributor to the death of StackOverflow. I think likely there are examples across many industries where this is the case. There's something that feels unjust about SO playing a role in their own demise, they didn't really consent to the answers ending up in training data. But I believe this is the wrong way to think about it.

Whether or not something seems fair isn't the most robust model for thinking about the world, it's better to just observe properties about information. You can keep things a secret. And you can share things with people that you trust and that's about all you can depend on in this world. Anything beyond this is a fools errand.

Trying to make information easily accessible *but only on your terms* is fairy tale thinking. Even in the more traditional, physical world of patents for inventions. China doesn't care, [Amazon doesn't care](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=scrub+daddy+alternative&adgrpid=186427414236&hvadid=779640091190&hvdev=c&hvexpln=0&hvlocphy=9004041&hvnetw=g&hvocijid=12507309215063347487--&hvqmt=e&hvrand=12507309215063347487&hvtargid=kwd-1372540675887&hydadcr=18708_13709482_2331412&mcid=ec97583ebd5032a79ff45de1423baee2&tag=googhydr-20&ref=pd_sl_134mg68bre_e). The right to any market edge is yours until someone figures out how you did something. It may not seem just, but again that's the wrong framing. It's just how it is.

I think a radical acceptance of this truth would lead to better outcomes for society as well as individuals.

For society, more people have access to greater opportunities when high quality information is not gate-kept. This should be obvious. *What about the poor pharma companies though? What incentivizes them to do the R&D if they aren't going to be protected?*.

We spend a lot of time debating healthcare costs, and I'm always dissapointed when people don't bring up the impact of patent monopolies
> [...] patent monopolies and related protections — are the reason many prices are high to begin with. Nearly all drugs, tests, vaccines, and medical equipment would be hugely cheaper without these monopolies. [source](https://cepr.net/publications/government-granted-monopolies-in-healthcare-create-need-for-prices-controls/)

Personally I would trade further R&D for massively reduced day-to-day healthcare costs. Pharma companies could still offset high research costs by trying to control distribution of medicine.
