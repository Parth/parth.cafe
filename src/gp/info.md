# Information is destined to be free
I propose that the most powerful property of information is for it's tendency to be as widely desseminated. To be clear, this is not a moral or ethical claim, though I will explore that as well. This is more of an observational claim. 

I spend a lot of time in entrepreneurial circles. And there's a cohort within these circles who are eager to spend precious early stage resources on things like patents. I think this is silly. For starters you don't know how valuable what you have is. You're burning resources on something that may never pay off. Resources that could be spent towards actually making that thing worth stealing. However if you think this doesn't apply to you, let me dissolution you of the power of patents. Enforcing a patent resource intensive, and possibly futile. Your copycat could just be in China. [Scrub Daddy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ae5MssJ8en4) the iconic Shark Tank alum has multiple design and utility patents. Amazon is literally a US company and it could not give two shits about the patents:

![pasted_image_2026-09-07_07-27-12.png|300](imports/pasted_image_2026-09-07_07-27-12.png)

There are many things that are patent shaped: copyrights, trademarks, ndas. Even in the realm where value is well established. It's very hard to produce something that can't be reverse engineered. Software, hardware, machines can all be reverse engineered relatively easily. Trying to prevent this behavior in most situations is [security through obscurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_through_obscurity) (a waste of everyone's time).

To be clear, there are secrets, you just keep them by only telling people you trust. It's not the NDA that's keeping the secret, it's choosing the right people.

A decade ago when information was slightly less free an Apple engineer left a prototype iPhone at a bar. Gizmodo (press) bought the iPhone from whoever found it for $5k and dropped the story. Apple aggressively tried to pursue legal action to prevent the story from being published, they tried to claim damages, etc. All futile. [1](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2010/04/iphone_lost_in_bar_prematurely.html) [2](https://www.pcworld.com/article/512394/gizmodo_police.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

The employee was likely under an NDA, who knows what happened to him. Most employers can fire you for whatever reason they want, so the NDA is moot. The iPhone probably had some sort of terms of service about authorized use, almost certainly moot. The thing that prevents the leak is not these structures, it's simply how aligned the incentives of the people that are holding the secrets.

Well wouldn't the NDA tip the scales of incentives? Not if you're a startup that has no resources. And realistically not if the secret or IP is valuable enough. The secret just has some value and likely there's an open market for the information, when the price is right, the secret's out.
